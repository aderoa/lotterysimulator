How the new system works

Ball allocation (16 lottery teams)

  • 10 non-playoff, non-play-in teams — 3 balls each, except the 3 worst overall records lose 1 ball ("draft relegation")
  • 3 worst overall records (relegated) — 2 balls each, with a guaranteed pick floor of #12
  • #9 and #10 play-in seeds (both conferences) — 2 balls each
  • Losers of the #7 vs #8 play-in games (both conferences) — 1 ball each. These teams may still make playoffs as the 8-seed

Floors

  • Draft-relegated teams cannot pick lower than #12
  • All other lottery teams cannot pick lower than #16

Own-pick restrictions

  • A team's own pick cannot be #1 in two consecutive drafts
  • A team's own pick cannot be top-5 in three consecutive drafts
  • Restrictions follow the originating team — even if the pick has been traded

Pick protections

  • Newly traded picks cannot use protections in the top-12 through top-15 band (must be unprotected, more aggressive, or less aggressive)

Modeling choices in this simulator

  • Records project from 2025-26 baseline using normal-distributed noise (std-dev scales with √yearsAhead) plus a configurable mean-reversion factor toward .500
  • Play-in 7v8 game: lower-record team is treated as the loser (use the play-in seeding from regular season). For ties, random
  • Lottery draws use weighted sampling without replacement (mathematically equivalent to the ball-combination system)
  • Floors are enforced by post-draw swaps — if a relegated team draws into 13-16, they swap with the highest-positioned non-relegated team within 1-12
  • Pick-restriction history must be entered manually (single-year sims don't track multi-year state)
